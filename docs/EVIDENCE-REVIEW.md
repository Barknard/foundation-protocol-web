# Foundation Protocol — Evidence Review (2026-06-17)

Auto-generated from an 8-topic web-research + expert-critique workflow.

## Summary

Foundation Protocol gets the big strategic calls right: session-level spike control (+10% over longest recent run, per the 2025 BJSM 5,200-runner cohort) instead of the discredited weekly-10%/ACWR rules; strength from week 1 as the primary injury-prevention lever (Lauersen 2018); a conservative run-walk start for a high-risk 40+ novice population; and effort/readiness-based autoregulation that only raises load when work was done AND recovery is good. Keep all of that. The high-severity gaps are: (1) tendon/bone pacing — running can begin on only ~8 weeks of genuine high-strain loading when >=12 weeks is needed, with no dedicated high-strain tendon block, so add a named Tendon Prep block in P1 and gate P2 on cumulative loading weeks, not a calendar date; (2) no deload — add an auto lighter week (volume -40-50%, not a full stop) every ~4-6 weeks, since a full week off can blunt strength; (3) no detraining model — add gap-tiered return ramps that restart RUNNING more conservatively than strength because tendon stiffness de-adapts in ~2 months while muscle/neural strength snaps back, the core 40+ reinjury trap; (4) RICE -> replace with PEACE & LOVE plus a two-bucket acute-vs-tendinopathy triage and a clinician red-flag screener; (5) Phase-4 daily 100/100/100 is high-volume near-failure work that contradicts the program's own RIR-2-3, ~10-set-ceiling, 48-72h-recovery logic — replace it with the evidence-based template plus power and bone-snack components. Medium items: make RIR 2-3 the explicit default with double-progression (don't advance load on a single good day), and split the single readiness item into sleep + soreness + feel so the app can deload the right thing. Net: the architecture is sound and unusually well-evidenced; the fixes are mostly about pacing the slowest tissue, building in recovery, and handling the two states (layoff, injury) the current logic ignores — all implementable inside the existing two-tap autoregulated model."

## Strengths to keep

- Session-level spike control as the primary running governor (+10% over longest run in last 30 days) is correctly aligned with the strongest current evidence (Johansen/Nielsen BJSM 2025, 5,205 runners, mean age 46). The program already governs at the session level rather than relying on the discredited weekly 10% rule or ACWR — keep this as the core running-safety engine.
- Strength built in from week 1 (P1 onward, 2x/wk full-body) before and alongside running. This is the single best-evidenced injury-prevention lever (Lauersen BJSM 2018: ~50% reduction in overuse injuries, ~66% overall, dose-dependent). Treating strength as non-optional core rather than an add-on is exactly right for a 40+ beginner.
- Run introduction is genuinely conservative: 2x/wk, run-walk starting 1:4 (1 min run / 4 min walk x5), non-consecutive implied. This matches the evidence that novice/40+ runners are in the highest-risk band (Videbaek 2015: novices 17.8 vs 7.7 injuries/1000h; Fokkema C25K 2023: 19% injured, mean age 47) and need more walk intervals and lower starting volume than generic plans.
- Autoregulation by daily readiness + 'did you do the work' is conceptually sound and evidence-supported. Effort/readiness-based progression (RIR/RPE, HRV-guided scheduling) matches or beats fixed percentage plans (Greig Sports Med 2022; network MA 2025 SUCRA RPE 66.8% vs percentage 13.2%; Granero-Gallegos 2021). The rule that load only rises when the user did the work AND feels recovered is a good fatigue gate.
- Front-loaded tissue-prep phase (P0: 4 weeks daily walking + daily PT for hip/glute/calf/balance, no strength load, no running) respects that connective tissue and bone lag cardio. Starting with low-impact base and balance/single-leg work is consistent with NSCA older-adult and ACSM multicomponent guidance and the bone-stress 'first month is the danger zone' finding (Warden framework; Nielsen JOSPT 2014).
- Protein target of 1.6 g/kg/day is within/at the top of the evidence-based range for active 40+ adults (PROT-AGE 2013; Phillips Adv Nutr 2018 recommend ~1.2-1.6), supporting muscle retention against age-related anabolic resistance and sarcopenia. Keep it, and add per-meal coaching (see issues).
- Polarized ~80/20 easy/hard distribution in P3 is a reasonable, injury-protective default. Even though the precise split matters less for novices than total easy volume (Rosenblat MA; Festa 2020), an easy-biased distribution protects against burnout/injury and is defensible.
- Sharp-pain flag immediately routing to Rest is a correct hard stop, and a daily symptom check-in that can hold/regress the plan is exactly the pain-gating mechanism the bone-stress and tendinopathy literature calls for (Nielsen JOSPT 2014; Silbernagel pain-monitoring model).

## Issues + changes

### 1. (high) Tendon/bone adaptation pacing vs. running start

- **Current:** P0 is 4 weeks (walking + light PT, no loaded strength). Loaded strength starts week 5 (P1). Running (run-walk) starts week 13 (P2), i.e. ~8 weeks after meaningful strength loading begins.
- **Problem:** Tendon stiffness needs a MINIMUM 12-week high-strain loading block before meaningful running mileage, and >=12 weeks is materially better than 8 (Bohm/Mersmann/Arampatzis 2015: stiffness ES 0.91 at >=12wk vs 0.81 at ~8wk). The P0 PT block uses LOW loads (bodyweight balance, band walks, calf raises) which do essentially nothing for tendon stiffness (low-intensity ES ~0.04). The clock on tendon conditioning effectively starts at week 5 when real load begins, giving only ~8 weeks of genuine high-strain exposure before running — and even that strength work is general, not a deliberate high-strain tendon protocol. Muscle/cardio will feel 'ready' weeks before tendon and bone are (Svensson/Heinemeier 2016; bone remodeling ~3-4 months, BMD gains 6+ months).
- **Change:** Add a named, trackable 'Tendon Prep' block embedded in P1 (weeks 5-12) targeting the Achilles/patellar/calf: heavy slow calf raises and loaded squat/leg-press holds, progressing toward ~3-second holds at high load, 3-4x/week, run concurrently with general strength. Hard-gate the start of P2 run-walk on completion of >=12 cumulative weeks of high-strain lower-limb loading (not a calendar date) AND a symptom-clear lower limb. Keep early P2 sessions biased to higher reps / lower peak impact. Surface in-app: 'feeling fit is not the same as connective-tissue ready.'
- **Rationale:** Bohm/Mersmann/Arampatzis (Sports Med Open 2015): tendon stiffness gains need high strain (>70% MVC, ES ~0.90) and are materially better at >=12 weeks; low load does nothing. Mersmann/Arampatzis (Front Physiol 2020): muscle strength can outpace tendon stiffening, lowering the tendon safety factor and raising tendinopathy risk — do not scale running off how fit/strong the user feels. Bone (ACSM Position Stand; Hannam 2017): multi-month lag, first month of new stimulus is highest BSI risk.

### 2. (high) No deload / recovery weeks anywhere in 40 weeks

- **Current:** Program explicitly has NO scheduled deload or recovery weeks across all 5 phases. Fatigue management relies entirely on daily autoregulation (feel<=1 -> Rest, feel==2 -> Modify).
- **Problem:** Daily readiness catches acute bad days but does NOT catch slow-accumulating fatigue, which is precisely what unscheduled deloads exist to dissipate. For a 40+ audience training 8+ months continuously, accumulated fatigue masks fitness (looks like a plateau/decline) and raises injury risk. The evidence does NOT support a full week off (Coleman/Burke PeerJ 2024: a 1-week full break slightly reduced lower-body strength) — but it DOES support a lighter back-off week every 4-8 weeks implemented as reduced volume/intensity, not cessation (Bell et al. 2024/2025).
- **Change:** Insert an automatic LIGHTER week (not a rest week) every ~4-6 weeks within P1-P4: cut working-set volume ~40-50% and back off one RPE point / drop running volume ~40%, keep frequency and movement patterns. Also let it trigger EARLY if the autoregulation trend shows under-recovery (e.g. >=3 'Modify/Repeat' calls or feel<=2 in a rolling 7-day window). Frame it in-app as 'load reduction to unmask fitness,' never as 'time off.'
- **Rationale:** Bell et al. (2024/2025) and Frontiers Sports Act Living 2022: deloads are conventionally every 4-8 weeks via reduced load/volume/frequency to dissipate fatigue. Coleman/Burke (PeerJ 2024): a full 1-week stop blunted strength with no upside — so use a lighter week, not cessation. This is a default safety valve for 40+ under-recovery, and pairs with the existing autoregulation as a slow-fatigue backstop daily readiness can't provide.

### 3. (high) No detraining / return-from-layoff handling

- **Current:** Program has no detraining or layoff model. After any break, the user presumably resumes at the session pointer where they left off (same load), since only a Progress call moves the pointer and nothing reduces it.
- **Problem:** Resuming at pre-break load after a 1-3 month layoff is the single most dangerous gap for a 40+ user. Muscle/neural strength returns FAST but tendon stiffness de-adapts within ~2 months and lags on retraining — exactly the mismatch that causes tendinopathy/rupture (Kubo 2010). Conversely, the app may also over-react to SHORT breaks: a 1-2 week break costs essentially no real strength (gains are partly neural; myonuclei retained) and should resume at the same load, but a naive 'restart' would needlessly demotivate and trigger dropout. The program does neither correctly because it has no model at all.
- **Change:** Add a gap-tiered return ramp keyed on days since last completed session (see detrainingModel field): <=2 wk resume same load; 2-4 wk drop ~10-15%, rebuild over ~1 wk; 4-8 wk start ~70% load, +~10%/wk, bias higher-reps/lower-impact, throttle running to early-P2 intervals; >8 wk start ~60% load, reintroduce running via run-walk regardless of prior level, ramp ~4-8 wk (≈ half the layoff). Always restart RUNNING more conservatively than strength because VO2max/strength return before tendon stiffness. Surface 'muscle memory' messaging to convert lapsed users.
- **Rationale:** Kubo et al. (JSCR 2010): tendon stiffness falls toward baseline by ~2 months detraining while muscle strength holds — the core 40+ injury risk on return. Detraining review/Stronger By Science + older-adult studies: 1-2 wk break ~no strength loss; VO2max drops fast (4-14% <4wk) so restart cardio easier (Zheng 2022). Myonuclear permanence + epigenetic memory (Cumming J Physiol 2024; Seaborne): regain is faster than first build; retraining restores in ~half the layoff duration (Fatouros/Kalapotharakos 2008).

### 4. (high) Injury handling is RICE, not PEACE & LOVE; no overuse/acute triage; no red-flag screener

- **Current:** Injury handling is just 'Rest'; a RICE protocol is being added. No distinction between acute strain/sprain and overuse tendinopathy. No red-flag routing to a clinician.
- **Problem:** RICE is no longer the standard — its own originator (Mirkin) retracted the rest/ice emphasis in 2015, and current consensus is PEACE & LOVE (Dubois/Esculier BJSM 2019/2020): protect + early optimal loading, NOT prolonged rest and ice. Ice has evidence only for short-term analgesia and may blunt the inflammation needed for repair; prophylactic NSAIDs can impair muscle regeneration (especially relevant for 40+ on BP/cardiac/renal meds). Worse, acute strains and overuse tendinopathy need OPPOSITE management — tendinopathy is a load/failed-adaptation problem that responds to progressive loading, not rest/ice. And there is no red-flag screener, which is essential safety scaffolding for a 40+ audience with higher fracture/serious-tear and comorbidity risk.
- **Change:** Replace RICE with the two-bucket protocol in the injuryProtocol field: (a) sudden acute event -> PEACE (Protect/offload briefly, Elevate, Avoid anti-inflammatories+ice as a healing step, Compress, Educate) then LOVE (Load, Optimism, Vascularization, Exercise) with graded reloading in days, not weeks; (b) gradual-onset load-related tendon pain -> isometric holds for flares then heavy slow resistance 2-3x/wk, governed by the Silbernagel pain-monitoring rule (pain <=3-5/10 during/after, settles to baseline within 24h, morning symptoms no worse). Add a red-flag screener at injury logging that halts self-rehab and routes to a clinician on any flag.
- **Rationale:** Dubois & Esculier (BJSM 2019/2020) PEACE & LOVE; Mirkin 2015 retraction. Mackey & Kjaer (2017): NSAIDs suppress satellite-cell repair. Functional > immobilization for sprains (umbrella review 2022; MA 2025). Cook/Purdam continuum + tendinopathy load-management: tendinopathy needs progressive loading, not rest. Silbernagel (AJSM 2007) pain-monitoring model. Ottawa Ankle Rules + red flags route fracture/serious-tear risk to care.

### 5. (high) Phase 4 '100/100/100 daily' (OPM-style) is high-volume, near-failure, anti-evidence for 40+

- **RESOLVED 2026-06-20:** capstone test now uses a 2-minute plank to match the trained anti-extension core (the 100-situp historical analysis below is retained for context).
- **Current:** P4 (wk41+) prescribes 'OPM' 100 pushups / 100 situps / 100 squats split across the day, building to 10K, plus loaded strength.
- **Problem:** 100 daily reps of each, every day, is the high-volume/low-return/high-injury zone the strength evidence explicitly warns against. (1) Daily high-rep work on the same patterns violates ~48-72h recovery and pushes weekly set volume far past the ~10-20 set diminishing-returns ceiling into the no-added-benefit / higher-injury zone (>20-30 sets). (2) 100 reps means training near or to failure, which adds nothing to strength/hypertrophy/power and increases joint/connective-tissue strain and form breakdown (ACSM 2026: leave 2-3 RIR). (3) 100 daily situps is high-volume repetitive lumbar flexion — poor risk/reward for a 40+ spine. (4) It abandons the RIR/autoregulation logic that governs the rest of the program. Simultaneously building to 10K plus daily 100s stacks fatigue with no deload.
- **Change:** Replace daily 100/100/100 with the program's own evidence-based template extended: full-body strength 2-3x/wk at RIR 2-3, ~2-3 heavy sets per key lift, total ~6-12 (cap ~20) hard sets/muscle/week, 48-72h between hard sessions for a muscle group, with a power component (sit-to-stand / fast concentric at 30-70% load). Swap 100 daily situps for loaded/anti-extension core (planks, dead bugs, carries — already in the program) and brief impact 'bone snacks' (a few sets of hops) spread across the week. Keep the 10K build but cap session spikes at +10% and keep the deload cadence. If a daily 'movement snack' habit is desired, make it sub-maximal greasing-the-groove (high RIR), not 100 reps to fatigue.
- **Rationale:** ACSM Position Stand 2026 (PMC12965823): training to failure adds nothing, prescribe RIR 2-3; Pelland et al. (Sports Med 2025): hypertrophy diminishing returns past ~10-11 sets/session and no benefit beyond ~30 wk sets, strength plateaus by ~2 direct sets/session. Doma et al. (Sports Med Open 2023): eccentric/high-volume work prolongs recovery; default 48-72h. Bone responds to short frequent impacts, not volume dumps (ACSM Bone Position Stand). NSCA Fragala 2019: include velocity/power work for fall risk.

### 6. (medium) Strength prescription lacks explicit RIR cap and double-progression rule

- **Current:** Strength days describe 'a hard set or two at ~80% 1RM' on multi-joint lifts. Progression is driven by the autoregulation Progress call (advance one session) when done & feel>=4.
- **Problem:** Two gaps. (1) No explicit RIR/leave-reps-in-reserve cue is specified in the working-set prescription — the most important safety lever for 40+. Without an explicit 'stop with 2-3 reps left' default, '~80% 1RM hard set' invites grinding to failure, raising joint/connective strain and form breakdown for zero added benefit. (2) The progression engine advances the session pointer on a single good day rather than confirming the load is tolerated; conservative individualized progression should require completing prescribed reps at target RIR for ~2 consecutive sessions before adding load, and only one FITT variable at a time.
- **Change:** Make RIR 2-3 the explicit default on every working set with a plain-language cue ('stop when you feel you could do about 2-3 more good reps'). Replace single-day pointer advance for LOAD with double-progression: add ~2.5-5% load or one set only after the user hits prescribed reps at RIR 2-3 for 2 consecutive sessions; never advance more than one variable per week. Offer a deconditioned on-ramp starting at 10-15 reps / light-moderate load (high RIR) before graduating to 6-12 reps heavier.
- **Rationale:** ACSM Position Stand 2026 (PMC12965823): failure adds nothing; default RIR 2-3 — single most important 40+ safety lever. ACSM Progression Models (2009/2026): conservative one-variable-at-a-time, tolerance-gated double progression. ACSM older/frail guidance: 10-15 rep light-load on-ramp is effective and safer; load is not the limiting variable when volume is matched.

### 7. (medium) Single daily readiness item is too thin to gate training

- **Current:** A single daily readiness rating (Q2: 1 wrecked .. 5 great), described as a simplification of the weekly 4-item Hooper Index. Sharp-pain flag and feel<=1 route to Rest.
- **Problem:** A single global 1-5 item conflates distinct recovery signals (sleep, soreness/muscle, stress/mood, fatigue) and will miss the specific drivers the evidence says matter most — sleep loss and eccentric/DOMS-type soreness. Sleep loss alone cuts physical performance ~7.6% and blunts training adaptation (Craven Sports Med 2022); muscle-damaging soreness is the dominant moderator of prolonged recovery in older adults (Doma 2023). One number can't distinguish 'sore legs, sleep fine' (modify lower-body) from 'slept 4h, not sore' (ease intensity globally).
- **Change:** Keep the two-tap simplicity but split readiness into 2-3 quick taps: (a) sleep last night (good/ok/poor), (b) muscle soreness (none/some/a lot, with optional body-area), plus the existing overall feel. Use them specifically: poor sleep -> auto-reduce intensity/volume that day (deload the session); high localized soreness -> Modify that muscle group / swap to easy aerobic or mobility, not necessarily a full Rest. Optionally fold in a phone-camera resting HR/HRV reading on low-readiness days. This stays simple while restoring the discriminating power of the Hooper-style multi-item check.
- **Rationale:** Craven et al. (Sports Med 2022, n=959): acute sleep loss cut physical performance ~7.6%, impairs recovery/anabolic signaling. Doma et al. (Sports Med Open 2023): eccentric/high-volume soreness is the strongest moderator of older-adult recovery length. Granero-Gallegos 2021: readiness-based (incl. HRV) scheduling matches fixed plans with fewer over-reaching days. Hooper 1995: multi-item (sleep/stress/fatigue/soreness) outperforms a single global score.

### 8. (medium) Missing dedicated power/velocity training and impact 'bone snacks'

- **Current:** Strength is slow multi-joint work (goblet squat, RDL, OH press, Bulgarian split squat, pushup, row, planks/dead bug). No explicit power/velocity component and no programmed impact loading until running starts.
- **Problem:** Velocity-based function (rate of force development) declines fastest with age and is a key fall-risk and functional-independence factor — slow strength work alone under-trains it (NSCA Fragala 2019; ACSM 2026). Separately, bone responds best to short, frequent, high-impact bouts (~>4g, ~60 impacts/day) rather than the eventual long runs, and bone lags muscle/cardio by months — so waiting until P2/P3 running to load bone delays adaptation and concentrates impact into longer sessions.
- **Change:** Add a light, fast power component to strength days for 40+: e.g. fast-concentric sit-to-stand / light goblet squats / step-ups moved with intent at ~30-70% load, low volume (sets x reps <=24), emphasis on speed not grind. Introduce brief, gamifiable 'bone snacks' (a few sets of hops/low jumps, building from gentle) spread across the week starting in late P1, well before running — short frequent bouts, not a volume dump.
- **Rationale:** NSCA Fragala et al. (JSCR 2019): older adults need dedicated power training (~40-60% 1RM, high velocity) for function and fall risk; ACSM 2026 agrees (power 30-70% 1RM, reps x sets <=24, emphasis on lifting speed). ACSM Bone Position Stand + Hannam 2017: bone responds to short frequent high-impact bouts (>4g, ~60/day), needs months and lags muscle/cardio — spread impact across the week rather than dumping into long runs.

### 9. (low) Warm-up not specified; risk of static stretching being treated as injury insurance

- **Current:** P0 PT block includes a calf stretch; no explicit pre-session warm-up structure is described, and no statement on stretching's role.
- **Problem:** If static stretching is positioned (even implicitly) as warm-up or injury prevention, the evidence is against it: pre-exercise static stretching acutely reduces explosive strength and does not reliably reduce all-cause injury. Dynamic warm-up improves performance and is associated with lower injury incidence. The included calf stretch is fine as mobility but shouldn't anchor the warm-up.
- **Change:** Specify a short (5-10 min) dynamic-movement warm-up before strength and run sessions (e.g. leg swings, bodyweight squats, ankle rocks, easy marching). Move any static stretching (including the calf stretch) to post-session or separate mobility days. Make clear in copy that strength training — not stretching — is the injury-prevention tool.
- **Rationale:** Warm-up network MA (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 2023): ~7-10 min dynamic work improves explosive output; static stretching acutely reduces it; dynamic warm-ups associated with lower injury. Lauersen (BJSM 2018) + stretching reviews: strength reduces injury ~66%; stretching alone does not reliably reduce all-cause injury.

### 10. (low) Protein coaching is daily-total only; misses per-meal anabolic threshold and creatine

- **Current:** Protein is a daily target of 1.6 g/kg/day. No per-meal distribution coaching, no leucine guidance, no creatine recommendation.
- **Problem:** Age-related anabolic resistance means daily totals alone under-deliver: older muscle needs ~0.4 g/kg/meal (vs 0.24 young) and a ~3 g leucine per-meal trigger, and the meal most under-dosed by older adults is breakfast. A daily-total-only nudge won't surface a leucine-light breakfast. Creatine monohydrate is a cheap, safe, evidence-based adjunct that amplifies the resistance training the user is already doing, and isn't mentioned.
- **Change:** Keep 1.6 g/kg/day but add per-meal coaching: target ~0.4 g/kg (≈25-40 g) across 3-4 meals, flag a leucine-light breakfast and suggest a leucine-dense top-up (dairy/whey, eggs, lean meat, soy). Add an optional creatine monohydrate recommendation (3-5 g/day, no loading) once the user is consistently resistance training, with a standard 'check with your doctor if you have kidney disease' note.
- **Rationale:** Moore/Phillips (Adv Nutr 2018, PMC5952928): ~0.4 g/kg/meal optimum in older adults; Zaromskyte (Front Nutr 2021): ~3 g leucine per-meal trigger; breakfast typically under-dosed. Creatine MA in aged adults (Eur Rev Aging Phys Act 2025): +lean mass SMD ~0.27, +strength SMD ~0.29; ISSN (Kreider 2017): 3-5 g/day, no loading, safe long-term.

## Detraining / return-from-layoff model

- **0-14 days (vacation, short illness, travel)** — Resume at the SAME load and same session pointer — no reduction. Keep one easy Zone-2 cardio session during the break if possible. On return, do one slightly easier 'reentry' strength session at RIR 3-4 before normal loading. (_1-2 week breaks cost essentially no real strength — early gains are partly neural and myonuclei/fiber size are retained (detraining time-course reviews; Stronger By Science 2023). Framing short breaks as harmless prevents all-or-nothing dropout. Cardio decays fastest (Zheng 2022), so a single easy session protects the aerobic system._)
- **15-28 days** — Reduce strength load ~10-15% and rebuild to prior over ~1 week (one good session at target RIR -> restore load). Restart cardio at reduced duration/intensity. Hold running at the last tolerated run-walk ratio rather than the most advanced one reached. (_Strength loss begins accelerating after ~2 weeks, but is modest; VO2max has dropped more (4-14% under 4 weeks) so cardio restarts easier (Zheng 2022). A small load haircut respects early tendon de-adaptation while muscle is largely intact (Kubo 2010)._)
- **29-56 days (1-2 months)** — Start strength at ~70% of pre-break load, add ~10%/week, and BIAS early sessions toward higher reps / lower peak load. Throttle running back to early-P2 run-walk intervals regardless of prior level. Reintroduce impact gradually (bone snacks before continuous running). Expect to be back near baseline in ~3-4 weeks. (_This is the danger window: tendon stiffness falls toward baseline by ~2 months while muscle/neural strength returns fast — load too quickly and the tendon can't handle what the muscle can lift (Kubo 2010; Mersmann/Arampatzis 2020). Higher-reps/lower-peak-load reconditions tendon before chasing strength._)
- **57+ days (>2 months)** — Start strength at ~60% of pre-break load, +~10%/week. Restart RUNNING via the P2 run-walk progression from the beginning regardless of prior level, gated by the +10% session cap and symptom check. Plan a ~4-8 week return-to-baseline ramp (≈ half the layoff length). Surface 'muscle memory' messaging: what you built comes back much faster the second time. (_Bone needs months and BMD lags; tendon stiffness is well below trained level. But myonuclear permanence and epigenetic memory mean reclaim is far faster than the first build (Cumming 2024; Seaborne), and retraining restores losses in ~half the layoff duration (Fatouros/Kalapotharakos 2008). Restart cardio/running conservatively because VO2max and strength outpace tendon readiness on the way back._)

## Injury protocol

- **Acute:** For a sudden event (rolled ankle, tweaked muscle, sharp pull) use PEACE & LOVE, not RICE. First days (PEACE): Protect — briefly offload the painful movement (relative rest, keep moving everything else; light support/brace only for ~7-10 days if needed); Elevate; Avoid anti-inflammatories and routine icing as a 'healing' step (ice only as an optional 15-20 min comfort tool in the first hours, never gating return); Compress; Educate (gentle early loading beats prolonged rest). After the first days (LOVE): Load — reintroduce movement and progressive loading within a few days as pain allows; Optimism; Vascularization (easy pain-free cardio); Exercise (graded strength back toward normal). Functional early mobilization returns people to activity faster than immobilization with no downside (umbrella review 2022). Acetaminophen/paracetamol is preferred over NSAIDs for pain; NSAIDs only sparingly for intolerable pain and check with a pharmacist/clinician (40+ users often on BP/cardiac/renal meds).
- **Overuse:** For gradual-onset, load-related tendon pain (Achilles, patellar/knee, elbow, gluteal, rotator cuff), treat it as a loading problem, not an inflammation problem — do NOT prescribe rest, ice, or NSAIDs as the answer. Use progressive tendon loading: isometric holds first for painful flares (they give ~45 min of pain relief and let you keep loading), then graduate to heavy slow resistance / progressive strength 2-3x/week over ~12 weeks (for knee/patellar prefer heavy slow resistance over eccentric-only). Govern it with the pain-monitoring rule: pain during and 24h after loading should stay <=3-5/10 and settle to baseline by the next morning with no worse morning stiffness — if pain climbs with load or morning symptoms worsen, hold or regress. Adherence over 12 weeks beats the choice of specific protocol (Maetz 2023; Silbernagel pain-monitoring model 2007; Cook/Purdam continuum).
- **See a clinician:** A red-flag screener at injury logging should HALT the self-rehab flow and route to urgent/clinical care (GP, physio, urgent care) if ANY of these are present: inability to bear weight for 4 steps; bony point tenderness (e.g. over the ankle malleoli or base of the 5th metatarsal — Ottawa Ankle Rules); obvious deformity; rapid or large swelling; numbness or pins-and-needles; joint locking or giving way; a 'pop' with immediate loss of function; or no improvement after ~1-2 weeks of appropriate self-management. Zero Ottawa criteria implies <1% fracture risk, but any flag warrants evaluation — essential safety scaffolding for a 40+ audience where fracture, serious tear, and medication-interaction risks are higher.

## In-app session rationales

- **P0 Infrastructure (wk1-4): daily walking + 10-min PT** — We build the slowest tissue first — tendons and bone lag your heart and muscles.  
  Walking and balance/hip/calf drills lay an aerobic and movement-quality base while your connective tissue starts adapting. Your fitness will feel ready weeks before your tendons and bones are, so we start gentle on purpose (tendon stiffness needs ~12 weeks of loading; bone needs months — Bohm/Arampatzis 2015; ACSM Bone Position Stand).
- **P1 Foundation — strength days (wk5-12), 2x/wk full-body at RIR 2-3** — Strength is your best injury insurance — and you stop 2-3 reps short, not at failure.  
  Two full-body sessions a week covering squat/hinge/press/row is the single most-proven way to cut overuse injuries (about half — Lauersen 2018). Leaving 2-3 reps in the tank gives the same strength and muscle gains with far less joint and tendon strain (ACSM 2026).
- **P1 — Tendon Prep block (heavy slow calf/leg holds, ~3s, 3-4x/wk)** — Heavy, slow holds stiffen the tendons that running will pound.  
  It's the load — not eccentric vs concentric — that conditions tendons: heavy work with ~3-second holds drives stiffness, light reps do almost nothing (Arampatzis; Bohm 2015). We run this for at least 12 weeks before real mileage so your tendons keep pace with your fast-improving strength and cardio.
- **P1/P3 — Zone-2 easy cardio** — Easy, conversational effort builds the aerobic base you can sustain without breaking down.  
  Zone 2 is for high, low-injury volume and habit, not for maxing out fitness — keep it truly conversational. We layer a little harder work in later for VO2max (Storoschuk 2025; cardio-mortality dose-response, Kokkinos 2022).
- **P2 Run Introduction (wk13+): run-walk 2x/wk, +10% session cap** — Each run grows by at most ~10% over your longest recent run — no hero sessions.  
  The strongest current evidence says single-session distance spikes cause overuse injury, not gradual weekly buildup (Johansen/Nielsen BJSM 2025, avg age 46). Run-walk keeps impact low while your bones and tendons — still catching up — adapt; the first month of any new running is the highest-risk window.
- **P3 Build (wk25-40): 3 runs/wk, 80/20 easy/hard, strength up to 3x** — Mostly easy, a little hard — and most days non-consecutive to let tissue recover.  
  Keeping ~80% of running truly easy protects against burnout and injury while a small dose of harder work lifts fitness (Rosenblat; Festa 2020). We space hard sessions 48-72h apart because older tissue, especially after impact, recovers a bit slower (Doma 2023).
- **Lighter (deload) week, auto-inserted every ~4-6 weeks** — We cut the load — not stop — to let hidden fitness surface.  
  Dropping working-set volume ~40-50% and easing intensity for a week clears accumulated fatigue that a single good day can't reveal. A full week off can actually dent strength, so we reduce rather than rest (Bell 2024; Coleman/Burke 2024).
- **Daily two-/three-tap check-in (goal + sleep/soreness + feel)** — How you slept and how sore you are decide today's dose, not a fixed plan.  
  Poor sleep blunts your training and recovery (~7.6% performance drop after sleep loss — Craven 2022), and soreness flags tissue that's still repairing (Doma 2023). On rough days we make the session easier or swap it, so you keep showing up without digging a hole.
- **P4 Target — strength + power + bone snacks + 10K build** — Get faster and more durable with quality sets and quick movements — not 100 grinding reps.  
  Beyond ~10 hard sets per muscle there's no added benefit and more injury risk, and training to failure adds nothing (Pelland 2025; ACSM 2026). We add fast, light 'power' reps and brief hops to protect function and bone, which decline fastest with age (NSCA 2019).
